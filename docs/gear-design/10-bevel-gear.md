# Workstream 10 — Bevel gear math + `BevelGearFeature` (straight bevel)

Read `00-conventions.md` first. Depends on Workstream 1 only (shares no
construction code with any other gear type — see below). Straight bevel
only — spiral/Zerol/hypoid bevel variants are a separate, further-later
phase (a materially bigger leap, arguably harder than every other
workstream in this project combined). **This is the single highest-risk
workstream in the whole project** — spike before committing
`11-bevel-pair.md` or `06-dxf-export.md`'s bevel flat-pattern question to
depend on the approach working.

## Why this is structurally unlike every other gear type

Every other gear type builds a flat 2D tooth profile and turns it into a
solid via an operation this codebase already has some version of
(Extrude for external/internal/rack, Sweep/Loft for helical/herringbone).
A bevel tooth has no flat profile at all — its flank is a genuinely 3D
curved surface on a cone. The math and the OCCT construction are both
real, first-time work here.

## Math

New file `app/document/bevel_math.py` (OCCT-free, mirrors `gear_math.py`'s
split — see `00-conventions.md`).

- **Spherical involute**: a curve generated by rolling a plane on a base
  cone (rather than a base cylinder, as the planar involute does), living
  on the surface of a sphere centred at the cone apex.
- **Pitch cone half-angles from tooth counts and shaft angle**:
  `γ1 = atan(sin(Σ) / (N2/N1 + cos(Σ)))`, `γ2 = Σ − γ1` — the general
  form for **arbitrary shaft angle Σ** (not restricted to 90°). Reduces
  to the familiar `γ1 = atan(N1/N2)` at Σ=90° — use as a known-value test.
- Addendum/dedendum cone angles.
- Face-width bound relative to cone distance (too large relative to cone
  distance thins the tooth toward degeneracy near the apex) — non-
  blocking warning, not a hard limit.

**Test requirement**: real reference-value test suite against known
standard bevel gear dimensions — more important here than anywhere else
in this project, since there's no existing precedent in this codebase to
sanity-check the derivation against.

## OCCT construction

New file `app/document/bevel.py`. Sample the spherical involute at the
outer (back) cone and at the inner cone (back cone distance − face
width) — two genuine 3D space curves per tooth flank on the generating
sphere, not planar profiles — then build each flank as a ruled/lofted
surface between them. `BRepOffsetAPI_ThruSections` (already scoped for
`04-helical-herringbone-loft.md`'s Loft) may be reusable here — it isn't
strictly limited to planar cross-sections — but this is **genuinely
unconfirmed and needs its own spike**, not an assumption; self-
intersection risk on a tight cone is real. Full gear body assembly (N
teeth around the cone, flank surfaces stitched into a closed shell,
addendum/dedendum cone surfaces capping top and bottom) is real BRep
shell/solid construction from curved surfaces directly — closer to raw
kernel work than any other Feature in this codebase.

## `BevelGearFeature`

Six-part checklist (`00-conventions.md`). Parameters: module (back-cone
equivalent), tooth count, pressure angle, face width, backlash, profile
shift, and **pitch cone angle as a direct field** (a standalone bevel
gear doesn't know its future meshing partner, so it can't derive its own
cone angle the way `11-bevel-pair.md`'s pairing system will — that
workstream computes and sets it automatically when generating a pair
together). Anchored via `plane_ref: PlaneRef` (apex = plane origin,
primary axis = plane normal), defaulting to the fixed XY plane — see
`00-conventions.md`.

## Complexity/risk

The highest in this entire project. Every other hard item elsewhere in
this project found something existing to reuse — `PlaneRef`, `MergeMode`,
Pattern/Mirror's multi-body pattern, `RevolveFeature`'s angle convention,
`ExternalVertexReference`. Bevel construction largely can't: no
shell-from-curved-surfaces precedent exists anywhere in this codebase.
Budget real, dedicated spike time before committing `11-bevel-pair.md`
to depend on this approach working.
